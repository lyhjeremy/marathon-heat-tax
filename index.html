The Heat TaxBoston Marathon 2001 to 2014

Each degree of heat costs about a minute

I paired fourteen Boston Marathons with the temperature on the course that day. The field's median finish time goes up by about 1.0 minute for every °F: 3:45 on a 51°F day in 2006, 4:16 on an 81°F day in 2012.

The fourteen races

Race-day temperature is the mean of hourly readings from 10:00 to 16:00 near the course midpoint. The median is over finishers between 2 and 8 hours. The two races above 70°F are the two slowest fields.

How it was done

Results come from the public llimllib/bostonmarathon mirror of the official Boston Marathon results; temperatures come from the Open-Meteo historical archive. fetch_data.py downloads both, and analyze.py takes each year's median finish, fits it against temperature with scipy.stats.linregress, and draws the figures. Python, pandas, SciPy and matplotlib.

Limitations

Thirteen points can describe a relationship but can't prove one. Field size grew from 13,422 finishers in 2001 to 31,939 in 2014 and qualifying standards changed over the period. Humidity, sun and wind travel with temperature and were not modeled, so the slope is the cost of a warm race day, humidity and sun included.
